1. Introduction

A cascode amplifier stacks a common-source (CS) transistor M1 and a common-gate (CG) transistor M2. The input drives the gate of M1; M2 has a fixed DC bias on its gate and carries M1's drain current to the output. M2 acts as a "shield": it holds M1's drain voltage nearly constant.

Operating principle: M1 converts the input voltage into a current (gm1vin). M2 passes that current almost unchanged to the load, but raises the output resistance to about gm2ro2ro1 and keeps the voltage swing across M1's gate–drain capacitance small.

Importance: high gain, high output resistance and wide bandwidth in one stage: used in op-amp input/gain stages, current mirrors, RF low-noise amplifiers and high-speed front ends.

Prerequisites: MOSFET saturation and small-signal model (gm, ro, gmb), CS and CG amplifiers, Miller effect, DC biasing.

3. Working Principle

  1. DC: choose VG1 to set ID. Choose VB so that VX = VB − VGS2 ≥ Vov1 (M1 saturated) and Vout ≥ VB − Vth (M2 saturated).
  2. AC signal flow: vin → M1 (gm1vin) → node X → source of M2 (low impedance ≈ 1/gm2) → drain of M2 → load. Output is 180° out of phase with the input.
  3. Shielding: output changes appear at node X reduced by roughly gm2ro2, so M1's current barely changes: the output resistance rises.
  4. Assumptions: both devices in saturation, small-signal linear model, λ-model for ro, body effect neglected unless stated.

Advantages: very high Rout/gain, small Miller capacitance (wider bandwidth), better input–output isolation. Limitations: lost voltage headroom (Vov1 + Vov2 minimum at the output), a more complex bias, and an extra pole at node X.

Misconceptions: (1) The cascode does not raise gm: Gm ≈ gm1. (2) With a small resistive RD, the gain is almost the same as a CS stage (the gain benefit needs a high-impedance load). (3) M2's gate is a DC bias, not a signal input.

5. Equations and Derivations

Output resistance: short vin, apply vx at the output. M1 current ix flows through ro1, so VX = ixro1. M2: vgs2 = −VX, so ix = −gm2VX + (vx − VX)/ro2. Substitute and solve:

Rout = ro1 + ro2 + gm2ro1ro2 ≈ gm2ro2ro1  (Ω; with body effect gm2 → gm2 + gmb2)

Transconductance: Gm = iout(short)/vin ≈ gm1.

Av = −Gm(Rout ∥ RD) ≈ −gm1[(gm2ro2ro1) ∥ RD]

Limiting cases: RD ≪ Rout → Av ≈ −gm1RD. Ideal current-source load → Av ≈ −gm1gm2ro1ro2 ≈ −(gmro)² for identical devices. CS stage for comparison: Av = −gm(ro ∥ RD).

Miller effect: resistance looking into the source of M2 ≈ 1/gm2 (large ro2, load), so the gain of M1 is ≈ −gm1/gm2 (= −1 if identical):

CMiller,cascode ≈ Cgd1(1 + gm1/gm2) ≈ 2Cgd1   vs   Cgd(1 + gmRD) for CS

Headroom: Vout,min = Vov1 + Vov2 (with VB = Vov1 + VGS2). Gain in dB: 20 log10|Av|. Power: P = VDDID.

6. Solved Numerical Problems

Problem 1 (Easy) – output resistance and ideal-load gain

gm1 = gm2 = 2 mS, ro1 = ro2 = 50 kΩ (no body effect). Find Rout and Av with an ideal current-source load.
Rout = 50k + 50k + (2 mS)(50k)(50k) = 100 kΩ + 5 MΩ = 5.1 MΩ (a CS stage: only 50 kΩ).
Av = −gm1Rout = −(2×10−3)(5.1×106) = −10,200 ≈ 80.2 dB. Interpretation: ≈ 100× the CS output resistance.

Problem 2 (Moderate) – with a resistive load

Same transistors, RD = 10 kΩ.
Rtot = 5.1 MΩ ∥ 10 kΩ ≈ 9.98 kΩ → Av = −(2 mS)(9.98 kΩ) ≈ −19.96. CS: −2 mS × (50k ∥ 10k = 8.33 kΩ) = −16.7. Interpretation: cascode gives only about 20 % more gain here; its value shows with high-impedance loads.

Problem 3 (GATE-style, Difficult) – Miller capacitance

gm1 = gm2 = 4 mS, RD = 5 kΩ, Cgd1 = 20 fF, ro large. Compare the Miller capacitance at M1's gate in a CS stage and a cascode.
CS: A = −gmRD = −20 → CM = 20 fF × 21 = 420 fF.
Cascode: gain of M1 ≈ −gm1/gm2 = −1 → CM = 20 fF × 2 = 40 fF. Ratio ≈ 10.5. Shortcut: cascode Miller factor ≈ 2 irrespective of the overall gain.

8. Core Electronics Interview Preparation

Industry perspective: cascodes appear in op-amp gain stages and current mirrors, RF LNAs and optical-receiver front ends. Interviews for analog design, validation and test roles focus on Rout, headroom, bias and why gain collapses when a device leaves saturation.

9. Applications and Industry Relevance

ApplicationRole of the cascode
Telescopic and folded-cascode op-amps / OTAsHigh DC gain in a single stage; common in ADC and filter circuits
Cascode current mirrors, bias generatorsHigh Rout → accurate, supply-independent currents
RF low-noise amplifiers (LNAs)Better reverse isolation, lower Miller capacitance, higher bandwidth
Optical receivers / transimpedance front endsIsolates the photodiode capacitance from the gain node
Analog cells in mixed-signal ICsGain without extra stages, so less compensation

Design considerations: headroom at low VDD, bias of VB across PVT, device matching, extra pole at node X, noise from M2 (small). Test challenges: M1 slipping into triode over process or temperature corners; output swing limits. Roles: analog IC design, RF design, layout/validation, characterization engineer.
